# Transcripta — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches

### 1. Midnight Gold Editorial
Very dark navy editorial design with brushed-gold accents, audio-wave motifs, and premium service-card composition. It should feel like a boutique media studio: precise, confident, and high-trust.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Quiet Paper Studio
Warm ivory canvas, ink-black typography, and restrained bronze details inspired by a premium stationery and publishing house. The service would feel calm, crafted, and human.

**Probability:** 0.03

### 3. Signal Grid
A dark, technical interface built around waveform lines, modular blocks, and restrained electric accents. It would make the service feel fast, systematic, and production-ready.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach: Midnight Gold Editorial

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial luxury with broadcast-studio cues: deep navy fields, warm metallic highlights, precise typographic hierarchy, and controlled visual rhythm.

### Core Principles
1. Make the service legible in seconds: one message, one action, one proof point per section.
2. Use gold as a signal of value, not as decoration everywhere; pair it with quiet navy space.
3. Treat audio waveforms, document sheets, and speaker labels as a visual language for transformation.
4. Build trust through clarity: explicit pricing, delivery expectations, privacy language, and direct contact routes.

### Color Philosophy
The nearly-black navy creates a focused, studio-like environment where the gold feels earned and premium. Ivory text keeps the page readable while muted slate-blue surfaces prevent the dark palette from becoming flat. Signature gold is reserved for CTAs, pricing emphasis, dividers, and the wordmark.

### Layout Paradigm
An asymmetric landing page: an editorial hero with a left-aligned copy rail and a right-side visual composition, followed by staggered service blocks, a pricing rail, and a practical request path. Avoid a repetitive centered-card stack; use offsets, varied widths, and anchored dividers.

### Signature Elements
- Thin gold waveform traces used as section separators and subtle background texture.
- Numbered service markers and speaker-label chips that echo transcript formatting.
- Soft glassy navy panels with a single crisp gold edge or highlight.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like opening a well-made production brief: immediate, precise, and low-friction. CTAs should reveal their destination clearly—WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, or email—with no ambiguous buttons.

### Animation
Use short opacity and translate reveals as sections enter, restrained waveform shimmer in the hero, and 160–220ms hover states on cards and links. Avoid looping spectacle; movement should suggest signal processing and editorial precision. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use Cairo for Arabic display and body text, with Space Grotesk for the Latin wordmark and English micro-labels. Headlines are bold and compact; supporting copy is regular with generous line-height. Prices use a large tabular-feeling numeric treatment to improve scanning.

### Brand Essence
Transcripta is the dependable Arabic-first transcription studio for creators, educators, researchers, and teams who need recordings turned into clean, usable text without the friction.

**Personality:** precise, discreet, responsive.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and outcome-led. CTAs are warm but specific. Microcopy is reassuring and transparent, never inflated.

Example lines:
- «من الصوت والفيديو إلى نص جاهز للاستخدام.»
- «أرسل التسجيل، وخذ قرارك على سعر وموعد واضحين.»

### Wordmark & Logo
Keep the existing T-and-microphone mark as the brand anchor. Use the circular mark in navigation and the full TRANSCRIPTA lockup in the hero and footer. Do not redraw the symbol or replace it with a generic microphone icon.

### Signature Brand Color
**Transcripta Gold — #E8B94D**, used against **Midnight Navy — #07111F** with ivory text **#F4F0E8**.
